Lenny Rachitsky (00:01): Today I'm joined by Casey Winters, former Chief Product Officer at Eventbrite and growth lead at Pinterest and Grubhub. Casey, how do you know when a startup has truly achieved product-market fit?

Casey Winters (00:30): Product-market fit is not a feeling, and it's not survey scores like the Sean Ellis 40% rule, though those can be early directional signals. True PMF is demonstrated by cohort retention curves that flatten parallel to the x-axis. If your user retention curve asymptotically approaches zero, you do not have PMF, regardless of how fast top-of-funnel acquisition is growing. Retention is the bedrock of growth. When a cohort flattens out—say at 20% or 30% after 6 months and stays there for years—that baseline represents your real market fit.

Lenny Rachitsky (01:25): How should early-stage startups measure and improve user retention cohort curves?

Casey Winters (01:50): First, define your natural product frequency. If you are Uber or Slack, your frequency is daily or weekly. If you are Airbnb or TurboTax, your frequency is annual. Measuring a travel product on a 7-day retention metric is a fatal mistake. Once frequency is defined, plot cohorts by signup week or month. To improve retention, you don't optimize notifications or drip emails; you improve the core onboarding experience so that users reach the core action faster, and you build habits around product use cases.

Lenny Rachitsky (02:40): How do top marketplace startups solve the chicken-and-egg cold start problem?

Casey Winters (03:10): In marketplaces, you must constrain the problem geographically or by category. At Grubhub, we didn't launch nationwide; we conquered Chicago neighborhood by neighborhood. You almost always need to subsidize supply first because buyers will not stick around an empty marketplace. Once supply density in a single zip code reaches critical mass, buyer liquidity follows naturally, and the network effects kick in.

Lenny Rachitsky (04:00): What frameworks exist for diagnosing whether low growth is a top-of-funnel or retention problem?

Casey Winters (04:25): Look at the ratio of new users to returning active users over time. If acquisition is growing linearly but daily active users (DAU) flatlines, your leaky bucket is empty—that is a retention crisis. If retention curves are stable and flat, but growth has stalled, then you have saturated your initial acquisition loops and need new distribution channels or expansion into adjacent personas.
