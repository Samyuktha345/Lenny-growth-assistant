Lenny Rachitsky (00:01): Welcome Bob Moesta, co-creator of the Jobs to be Done (JTBD) framework alongside Clayton Christensen, and author of Demand-Side Sales 101. Bob, what advice do you give about using the Jobs to be Done framework?

Bob Moesta (00:30): People don't buy products; they "hire" them to make progress in a specific struggling moment. The biggest mistake product teams make is defining the job around their product category or feature set. You don't buy a drill because you want a quarter-inch drill bit; you buy it because you want a hole in the wall to hang a family picture. When you uncover the struggling moment and the emotion behind it, product trade-offs become crystal clear.

Lenny Rachitsky (01:25): What are the core forces at play when someone decides to switch from an existing solution to a new product?

Bob Moesta (01:55): There are four forces of customer progress. Two push them toward the new solution: the Push of the current situation (frustration with the status quo) and the Pull of the new solution (the magnetism of the better future). But two opposing forces hold them back: the Habit of the present (muscle memory and inertia) and the Anxiety of the new solution (fear of making a mistake, learning curve, risk). For a customer to switch, Push + Pull must be greater than Habit + Anxiety. If you don't reduce the anxiety and habit friction, the best feature set in the world will not generate adoption.
