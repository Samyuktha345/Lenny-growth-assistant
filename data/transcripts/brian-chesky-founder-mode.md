Lenny Rachitsky (00:01): Welcome to Lenny's Podcast. Today my guest is Brian Chesky, co-founder and CEO of Airbnb. Brian, you recently gave a talk at Y Combinator about what is now being called "Founder Mode". Can you explain what Founder Mode is and how it differs from conventional management?

Brian Chesky (00:45): The conventional wisdom taught in business schools and by venture capitalists is what I call "Manager Mode". Manager Mode says: hire great professional executives, give them autonomy, and get out of their way. You treat the company as a modular system where you delegate everything and only review high-level metrics. But what we discovered, and what Steve Jobs proved at Apple, is that when founders do that, the executives often end up running their own silos, optimizing for their own career goals, and the customer experience degrades.

Lenny Rachitsky (01:30): So what does Founder Mode look like in practice day to day?

Brian Chesky (02:00): Founder Mode means you stay in the details. You don't manage through layers; you inspect the actual work. At Airbnb, I review the product roadmaps directly. We got rid of traditional product managers who just wrote PRDs and didn't touch design. We combined product management with product marketing, and design became the center of the company. In Founder Mode, you have a direct line of sight to the people doing the work, not just the people managing the people.

Lenny Rachitsky (02:45): Isn't there a risk that people view that as micromanagement?

Brian Chesky (03:15): People only call it micromanagement when you are meddling without understanding the context. When Steve Jobs was reviewing every detail of the iPhone, nobody called it micromanagement—they called it excellence. In Founder Mode, you are not doing everyone's job for them. You are holding an uncompromising standard for quality, consistency, and customer delight across the entire product.
