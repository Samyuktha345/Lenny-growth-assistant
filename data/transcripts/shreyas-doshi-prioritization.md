Lenny Rachitsky (00:01): Welcome back, Shreyas Doshi. You are one of the most celebrated product thinkers in tech, having led teams at Stripe, Twitter, Google, and Yahoo. Shreyas, what strategies do you recommend for prioritizing high-impact product work?

Shreyas Doshi (00:35): Most product managers suffer from what I call "the prioritization delusion." They use complex RICE score spreadsheets that give an illusion of precision while masking flawed assumptions. I recommend the LNO framework: categorize all product tasks and initiatives into Leverage, Neutral, and Overhead. Leverage tasks have a 10x ROI—a small input creates massive asymmetric upside for the business or user. Neutral tasks are table stakes with 1x ROI; doing them exceptionally well does not change the outcome. Overhead tasks are low ROI (<1x) bureaucratic necessities. High-performing PMs aggressively protect their time to spend 60%+ on Leverage work and delegate or minimize Overhead.

Lenny Rachitsky (01:45): How should product leaders think about product sense and strategy versus pure execution?

Shreyas Doshi (02:15): Strategy without execution is hallucination, but execution without strategy is just high-speed failure. Product sense is empathy for the user combined with acute domain intuition. It is built by spending thousands of hours talking to real customers, analyzing telemetry data, and playing with competing products until you develop an instinctive taste for what will work.

Lenny Rachitsky (03:00): What are the best interview questions when hiring your first Head of Product?

Shreyas Doshi (03:30): Avoid generic behavioral questions. Instead, ask them: "Tell me about a time you took a contrarian bet on a product feature that turned out to be right, and what was your specific thesis when everyone else disagreed?" Another great question is: "Walk me through how you decided NOT to build something that your highest-paying customer was begging for." This reveals whether they have spine, strategic conviction, and customer empathy, rather than just being a feature-factory backlog administrator.
